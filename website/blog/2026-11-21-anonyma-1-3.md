anonyma 1.3 makes detection stricter where it can be and broader where it should be, lets several
regulations apply at once, and leaves signed evidence of what was done. It also comes with a command
line and this site.

<!-- truncate -->

## Check digits decide

A CPF, a CNPJ, a Spanish DNI or a French NIR has check digits. The validating pipelines now verify
them for thirteen national ID formats and leave numbers whose digits fail — test data, references,
typos — as they are. Thirteen new validators are available on their own too, including the
alphanumeric CNPJ that Brazil issues from July 2026, and patterns can name the one their matches
must pass.

A keyword a few words before a value now counts: `withContext()` raises the confidence of a match
after "CPF", "badge" or "password", or requires one.

## Brazil and Europe

Brazilian phone numbers, CNPJ, RG and PIX keys and payloads (verified by their CRC) are detected,
as are the national phone formats of France, the United Kingdom, Spain, Italy and the
German-speaking countries, and European and Brazilian street addresses.

## Several regulations at once

```ts
createAnonymizer({ preset: ["lgpd", "hipaa"] });
```

Every category of every preset is detected; each gets the most protective of their strategies.

## Receipts

`createReceiptIssuer()` signs what a run did — categories, counts, a digest of the output — without
a single value, and `verify()` tells whether a receipt was altered or issued for another output.

## The command line

```bash
anonyma scrub customers.csv -o customers.safe.csv --preset lgpd --receipt run.json
anonyma scan app.log.gz --fail-on-pii
```

Text, CSV and JSON Lines of any size, in constant memory.
